# Preprint Hosting Options for a High-School Researcher

## Best options for a biology or bioinformatics paper

Being a high-school student does **not automatically disqualify you from arXiv**. The usual obstacle is arXiv's endorsement system: a first-time submitter, or someone entering a new subject category, may need endorsement from an established arXiv author. An institutional email can sometimes simplify the process, but a university degree or faculty position is not itself required. See [arXiv's endorsement help page](https://info.arxiv.org/help/endorsement.html).

For a biology or bioinformatics paper, consider these platforms in the following order:

| Platform | Best use | Important requirements | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **bioRxiv** | Biology, genomics, bioinformatics, computational biology | A genuine affiliation; author approval; substantive original research; basic screening | **Best first choice** |
| **Research Square** | Broad scientific and medical research | Basic prescreening, manuscript metadata, declarations, primary institution | **Best general alternative** |
| **Preprints.org** | Multidisciplinary research | Basic screening; author information; institutional email recommended but not mandatory | **Good accessible fallback** |
| **Zenodo** | Archiving any scholarly work with a DOI | Minimal metadata; no conventional scientific screening | **Easiest archival option** |

## 1. bioRxiv — probably your best choice

For a genuine biology or bioinformatics research article, **bioRxiv is the most natural destination**. It is free, assigns a DOI, preserves manuscript versions, and performs basic screening rather than formal peer review. It does not use arXiv's endorsement system. See [bioRxiv's submission page](https://www.biorxiv.org/submit-a-manuscript).

Computational studies are accepted, so there is **no general requirement for wet-lab validation**. However, bioRxiv warns that routine or automated analysis of public datasets, simple database searches, sequence alignments, or molecular modeling without sufficient biological novelty may not qualify. Your manuscript should read as a complete research paper with a clear question, methodology, results, validation, biological interpretation, and contribution beyond merely rerunning an existing pipeline. See the [bioRxiv FAQ](https://www.biorxiv.org/about/FAQ).

The main complication for you is affiliation. bioRxiv asks authors to provide an affiliation so that an organization providing oversight can be contacted. You should:

- List your high school only when the school, a teacher, or a school program genuinely supervised or supported the work.
- List a research laboratory only when you actually worked with that laboratory and have permission to use the affiliation.
- Never invent or borrow a university affiliation.
- Contact bioRxiv before submitting if the project was completely independent and you are unsure whether "Independent Researcher" will be acceptable.

A teacher or adult mentor can help with the submission, but they should not be added as an author merely to overcome an account or affiliation requirement. Authorship should reflect a real intellectual or research contribution.

## 2. Research Square — strongest general alternative

**Research Square** accepts a broad range of scientific research articles. Direct submission is free, manuscripts undergo prescreening, and accepted preprints receive a DOI. Its instructions describe ordinary account registration, manuscript upload, institutional information, declarations, and prescreening; they do not describe an arXiv-style endorsement requirement. See [Research Square's information about submission costs](https://support.researchsquare.com/support/solutions/articles/6000271832-what-does-it-cost-to-submit-my-manuscript-to-research-square-).

It is especially reasonable when:

- The manuscript is interdisciplinary and does not fit neatly into bioRxiv's biological scope.
- bioRxiv considers the public-data analysis insufficiently substantive.
- Your eventual target journal is associated with Springer Nature, although Research Square is not limited to Springer Nature journal submissions.

Research Square preprints can be discovered through services including Crossref, Google Scholar, Europe PMC, ResearchGate, and others, although inclusion can depend on subject and indexing rules. See [Research Square's indexing information](https://support.researchsquare.com/support/solutions/articles/6000271865/).

For your situation, this would be my **second choice after bioRxiv**.

## 3. Preprints.org — accessible multidisciplinary option

**Preprints.org** is a broad, multidisciplinary preprint server. Submission is free, accepted manuscripts receive a DOI, and the service conducts basic screening before posting. Its instructions recommend an institutional email but do not make one universally mandatory. See [Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/).

This is a practical option when:

- You have difficulty satisfying bioRxiv's affiliation or scope screening.
- Your work crosses biology, computer science, mathematics, and statistics.
- You want a recognizable preprint record rather than merely uploading a file to a personal website.

Its audience is less specifically concentrated in computational biology than bioRxiv's, so **bioRxiv would normally provide better disciplinary context and visibility** for a biological research paper. That is a practical judgment rather than a guarantee about readership.

## 4. Zenodo — easiest way to obtain a permanent public record

**Zenodo** is a general-purpose research repository operated within the CERN/OpenAIRE ecosystem. A registered user can create a public record, upload a manuscript, and receive a DOI. It supports versioning and does not require scientific peer review or an arXiv-style endorsement. See [Zenodo's documentation about records](https://help.zenodo.org/docs/deposit/about-records/).

Zenodo is excellent for:

- Establishing a timestamped, citable public record.
- Hosting a manuscript together with code, supplementary tables, figures, or datasets.
- Preserving work that does not fit a conventional preprint server.

The disadvantage is that Zenodo is fundamentally a **repository**, not a biology-focused preprint community. It will host the paper reliably, but it generally provides less subject-specific screening and organic discovery than bioRxiv.

## What I recommend for your paper

Based on your description—a biology or bioinformatics paper with computational analysis and validation using another dataset—I would proceed as follows:

1. **Try bioRxiv first.** Present it as a complete research article, not merely as a dataset reanalysis. Emphasize the new method, biological conclusions, independent validation dataset, robustness checks, code availability, and what your study adds beyond prior work.
2. **Use Research Square if bioRxiv declines it** because of scope, affiliation, or the nature of the computational analysis.
3. **Use Preprints.org** if you need a more flexible multidisciplinary preprint server.
4. **Use Zenodo** if your primary goal is to obtain a stable DOI and publicly archive the manuscript with minimal gatekeeping.

I would not upload the same paper to all four. Both server guidance and common preprint practice favor choosing a single preprint home, because duplicate records create competing DOIs, fragmented citations, and confusion over which version is authoritative. See the [bioRxiv FAQ](https://www.biorxiv.org/about/FAQ).

## Important checks before posting

A preprint is effectively a permanent public disclosure. Even when a server permits withdrawal, the DOI and a withdrawal notice will generally remain. Therefore, make sure all coauthors approve the exact version, check your intended journal's preprint policy, remove confidential or personally identifying information, and consider patent implications before uploading. See [Research Square's withdrawal policy](https://support.researchsquare.com/support/solutions/articles/6000271859-can-i-withdraw-or-remove-my-preprint-from-the-platform-).

Because you may be under 18, have a parent, guardian, teacher, or legitimate research mentor review the particular platform's account terms before submission. One especially important detail is that ORCID's current terms say that a person under 18, or below the applicable age of majority, may not create an ORCID account. Therefore, do not create one merely because a submission form recommends ORCID; it is normally optional for preprint submission. See [ORCID's terms of use](https://info.orcid.org/terms-of-use/).

Also disregard older articles recommending the general **OSF Preprints** server: new submissions to the generalist OSF Preprints service were suspended beginning August 25, 2025, although several specialized community-run OSF preprint services remain active. See [OSF's preprint suspension notice](https://help.osf.io/article/687-osf-preprints-suspension).

## Overall recommendation

Submit to **bioRxiv** using an entirely accurate affiliation. If affiliation or scope becomes a problem, **Research Square** is the best next option; **Zenodo** is the reliable no-frills fallback.
