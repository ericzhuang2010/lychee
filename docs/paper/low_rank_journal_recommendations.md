# Journal recommendations for the unified manuscript

The best next target is **Plant–Environment Interactions**, followed by **G3**. Both are more compatible with a rigorous computational reanalysis than many nominally lower-impact plant-pathology journals.

The manuscript reanalyzes five public RNA-seq cohorts, applies multiple robustness checks, and identifies a small set of context-dependent candidates without new wet-lab validation. PeerJ's rejection was therefore primarily a policy-fit problem: it required reused datasets to be supported by comprehensive new experimental validation ([rejection email](peerj/rejection_email.txt)). Simply choosing the lowest IF would not necessarily solve that problem.

## Recommended shortlist

| Priority | Journal | 2025 JIF | Assessment |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | [Plant–Environment Interactions](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/25756265) | 1.9 | Best editorial fit. It evaluates scientific quality without requiring subjective novelty or predicted impact. It has also published a recent computational study integrating 112 transcriptomic samples and prioritizing genes for later functional validation ([example](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42344281/)). Main drawback: research articles are normally limited to about 3,000 words, so the current roughly 7,600-word manuscript would need substantial condensation. Fully OA; the current APC is listed [here](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/25756265/article_publication_charges). |
| 2 | [G3: Genes\|Genomes\|Genetics](https://academic.oup.com/g3journal/pages/about) | 2.4 | Very strong methodological fit. G3 explicitly avoids subjective novelty/impact criteria and covers transcriptomes, computational omics, agricultural genetics, and gene–environment interactions. It has published reanalyses of public genomic and transcriptomic datasets ([example](https://academic.oup.com/g3journal/article/6/8/2319/6054882)). Unlike PEI, its regular Investigation articles have no strict length limit. Fully OA with an APC. |
| 3 | [Plant Gene](https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/plant-gene/2352-4073) | ~2.3\* | Directly covers plant gene expression, functional genomics, biotic stress, plant–microbe interactions, and computational genomics. It accepts confirmatory and resource studies, but computational articles require biological, functional, or benchmarking validation. The manuscript's cross-method, leave-one-out, QC, and independent-cohort analyses may qualify as benchmarking validation, but an editorial inquiry before formatting is advisable. Hybrid journal, so the subscription route generally avoids an APC. |
| 4 | [Tropical Plant Pathology](https://link.springer.com/journal/40858) | 1.9 | Excellent organism and disease fit: lychee is a tropical crop and *P. litchii* is a major oomycete pathogen. It covers host–pathogen interactions and molecular plant pathology. The risk is that its novelty expectations appear more traditional and experiment-oriented. Hybrid; the [subscription route has no APC](https://link.springer.com/journal/40858/how-to-publish-with-us). |
| 5 | [Canadian Journal of Plant Pathology](https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/tcjp20/about-this-journal) | 1.8 | Appropriate sections include genomics/bioinformatics, host–pathogen interactions, and molecular pathology. It is a credible option, but its reported acceptance rate is only 18%, so it should not be treated as an easy fallback. Hybrid, with no mandatory APC for conventional publication. |
| 6 | [Journal of General Plant Pathology](https://link.springer.com/journal/10327) | 1.4 | Broadly covers molecular biology, genetics, and physiology of plant diseases. The IF is substantially lower, but there is less evidence that the journal actively welcomes public-data-only computational studies. Best used after an editorial inquiry. Hybrid; [no APC for the subscription route](https://link.springer.com/journal/10327/how-to-publish-with-us). |
| 7 | [Plant Protection Science](https://pps.agriculturejournals.cz/) | 1.0 | A lower-cost final fallback covering plant diseases and molecular plant protection. It has published transcriptional/gene-family analyses, but its fit with a multi-cohort reanalysis is less certain. Its current publication charge is approximately €565 plus applicable tax ([fee schedule](https://pps.agriculturejournals.cz/artkey/inf-990000-3900_Fees-CJFS.php)). |

## Special option if an impact factor is not required

[Discover Plants](https://link.springer.com/journal/44372) is a new, broad, sound-science journal covering plant genomics and biotic interactions. It currently advertises no APC for articles accepted through December 31, 2026. However, it does not yet list a Journal Impact Factor, Scopus, or Web of Science indexing on its journal page. It is pragmatic only if those credentials are not required by the authors' institution or funder.

## Journals to avoid despite their low IF

- [Journal of Phytopathology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14390434/homepage/forauthors.html), JIF 1.2: its guidelines state that purely descriptive studies without experimental insight will not be reviewed.

- [Archives of Phytopathology and Plant Protection](https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/gapp20/about-this-journal), JIF 1.2: its reported acceptance rate is only about 14%, and parts of its scope emphasize experimental or field validation.

- [European Journal of Plant Pathology](https://link.springer.com/journal/10658), JIF 2.1: topically suitable, but it explicitly emphasizes experimental approaches.

## Recommended submission sequence

1. Send brief presubmission inquiries to **Plant–Environment Interactions** and **G3**, attaching the abstract and explaining that the paper performs registered multi-cohort reanalysis with independent cross-context and sensitivity validation.

2. Choose PEI if the main text can be reduced substantially and APC coverage is available.

3. Choose G3 if retaining the complete analytical and reproducibility story is more important.

4. If there is no APC budget, approach **Plant Gene**, then **Tropical Plant Pathology**, using editorial inquiries before reformatting.

5. Keep **Journal of General Plant Pathology** and **Plant Protection Science** as later fallbacks.

The overall recommendation is **PEI first, G3 second, and Plant Gene third**. These choices address the actual rejection risk—acceptance of computational reanalysis without new wet-lab experiments—rather than optimizing IF alone.
