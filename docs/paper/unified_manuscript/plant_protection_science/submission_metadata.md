**Title:** Differences among lychee cultivars in responses to *Phytophthora litchii* nominate cell-wall and carbohydrate-associated candidates

**Suggested short title:** Cultivar-dependent lychee responses to *P. litchii*

**Abstract (173 words):**

Litchi downy blight, caused by the oomycete *Phytophthora litchii*, affects young leaves, inflorescences and fruit of lychee (*Litchi chinensis*). We assembled five public sequencing cohorts for discovery, cross-context evaluation and auxiliary analyses of cultivar-dependent infection responses. Discovery in Guiwei and Yurong1 leaves tested 19445 expressed genes. Two hundred and six genes passed the DESeq2 discovery rule and mapping and gene-model quality control; 16 remained stable under alternative quantification, statistical analysis, expression filtering, leave-one-library-out analysis and mapping checks. Twelve were prioritised in Tier B, with provisional annotations related to sugar transport, starch metabolism, cell-wall remodelling, lipid signalling and RNA metabolism. In an independent pericarp time course, LITCHI005518, a putative alpha-xylosidase, and LITCHI001963 showed same-direction cultivar-dependent effects, whereas five genes reversed direction. Neither externally supported gene passed all internal robustness criteria. The frozen 206-gene signature showed tissue-dependent infection responses in Feizixiao. Most of 18 earlier candidates responded within at least one cultivar, but none passed the genome-wide cultivar-interaction criterion. These results distinguish general infection responses from cultivar-dependent effects and nominate context-qualified candidates for experimental validation.

## Suggested cover-letter summary

This registered, multi-cohort analysis separates infection responses within lychee cultivars from formal cultivar-by-infection effects. Across five public sequencing cohorts, it identifies 206 quality-controlled candidates, 16 genes that remained stable across prespecified sensitivity analyses, 12 Tier B candidates and two genes with primary cross-context support. No gene met both complete internal robustness and primary external-support criteria. It also reports null and opposite-direction outcomes, shows strong context dependence and nominates provisional cell-wall and carbohydrate-associated hypotheses for experimental validation.
